SOIL TYPE

Loamy Soil

Loamy soil is a balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay. It retains moisture well and is rich in nutrients. Loam soil is a type of soil characterized by a balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay particles. It's generally considered the most ideal soil type for plant growth due to its excellent water-holding capacity, good drainage, and ability to provide sufficient aeration for roots

Best Crops:

  • Wheat
  • Barley
  • Potatoes

Sandy Soil

Sandy soil has larger particles and drains quickly. It is less fertile but warms up faster in spring. Sandy soil is a light, gritty soil made from weathered rocks that's high in sand and low in clay. It's often found in arid regions.

Best Crops:

  • Carrots
  • Radishes
  • Melons

Clay Soil

Clay soils contain more than 30 percent fine clay particles. Some clays swell and shrink as they wet and dry, which can add to soil fertility. Clay soils take longer to warm up in spring. Wet clay soils are easily damaged when dug or walked on. Drought is much less damaging on clay soils than others so.

Best Crops:

  • Rice
  • Beans
  • Peas

Silty Soil

Silty soil, characterized by particles smaller than sand but larger than clay, feels smooth and powdery when dry and slippery when wet, retaining water better than sand but less than clay.

Best Crops:

  • Corn
  • Sunflowers
  • Squash
